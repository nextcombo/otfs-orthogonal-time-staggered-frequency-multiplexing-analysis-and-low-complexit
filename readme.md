# [OTFS] Orthogonal Time-Staggered Frequency Multiplexing: Analysis and Low-Complexity Receiver Design [with MATLAB Code]

This paper proposes Orthogonal Time-Space Multiplexing (OTSM), a novel single-carrier modulation scheme that places information symbols in the delay-time domain and then performs successive operations of time-division multiplexing (TDM) and Walsh-Hadamard sequence multiplexing. With the help of Walsh-Hadamard transform (WHT), modulation and demodulation do not require complex field multiplication operations. For the proposed OTSM, we first derive the input-output relationship in the delay-time domain and propose a low-complexity detection method using zero-padding. Simulation results show that OTSM has higher performance gains compared to Orthogonal Frequency Division Multiplexing (OFDM), with similar performance to Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS), but with lower complexity due to the use of WHT. Subsequently, we introduce a low-complexity time-domain channel estimation method. Finally, we demonstrate how external error control codes and turbo decoders can be incorporated to improve the error performance of the encoding system.
In recent years, [4] has proposed Orthogonal Time-Frequency Space (OTFS) modulation, which shows significant advantages over OFDM in high mobility environments. OTFS places information symbols in the delay-Doppler (DD) domain to capture the channel geometric structure of mobile terminals and reflectors in analog high mobility scenarios. Using this representation, the OTFS modulator reuses each information symbol through two-dimensional orthogonal basis functions (an IFFT along the Doppler direction and a FFT along the delay direction) across the entire time-frequency domain required for transmitting one frame. The design of these basic functions is aimed at combating the dynamic characteristics of time-varying multipath channels. Moreover, [5] indicates that any two-dimensional orthogonal transformation (precoding) using constant modulus base functions in the time-frequency domain can enable receivers to fully utilize maximum time-frequency diversity. Since the Fourier basis has constant modulus properties, OTFS ensures that information symbols experience the same signal-to-noise ratio (SNR). In recent years, there has been significant effort invested in the development of OTFS (e.g., [5-21] and its references).
In this paper, we propose a novel single carrier modulation scheme: Orthogonal Time-Sequential Multiplexing (OTSM). The core idea is to reuse information symbols in the delay-domain rather than the OTFS's DD domain, where the rate is defined as the number of zero crossings per unit time interval. Specifically, OTSM will transform information symbols in the delay-rate domain to the delay-time domain, followed by time-domain signal transmission and reception. This domain transformation is realized by using the inverse Walsh-Hadamard transform (IWHT) along the order rate domain, instead of the IFFT used in the OTFS along the Doppler domain, as shown in Figure 1. In this manner, OTSM allows channel delay spread and Doppler spread to cause inter-symbol interference (ISI) along the delay and rate dimensions respectively, while maintaining separability at the receiver as in OTFS [4,17]. It should be noted that OFDM cannot achieve this degree of separation, as the channel delay spread and Doppler spread jointly cause interference along the frequency dimension in OFDM. Therefore, due to the loss of orthogonality between OFDM subcarriers, single-tap equalization fails. Our single carrier OTSM scheme only uses one-dimensional orthogonal WHT transformation, unlike the multicarrier OTFS scheme in [5], which employs arbitrary two-dimensional unitary transformations (such as DFT, WHT, and discrete long spherical sequence) in both time and frequency domains.
In response to the proposed OTSM modulation, we have derived its delay-order rate domain input-output relationship and proposed a low-complexity detection scheme. Similar to OTFS, we found that due to the constant mod WHT, the information symbols of OTSM experience approximately the same SNR at the receiver end. Therefore, OTSM can provide performance similar to OTFS in both static and high mobility channels, but with lower complexity because only addition and subtraction operations are required for WHT.
Additionally, we introduce zero-padding (ZP) between blocks in the time domain to avoid inter-block interference and reduce detection complexity while allowing for the insertion of pilots for channel estimation. Subsequently, we propose a low-complexity time-domain channel estimation method based on reconstructing delay - time-domain channels from time-domain pilots. We compare the performance of OTFS and OTSM using the delay-time-domain channel reconstruction method and find that their performances are similar. Finally, we demonstrate how external error correction codes can be used to improve error performance and reduce the required pilot power for accurate detection.
Overall, the proposed OTSM modulation has the following advantages. The OTSM transceiver is low in complexity (favorable for hardware implementation), yet it achieves the benefits of multi-carrier systems without sacrificing performance. Similar to OTFS, OTSM can easily be stacked on top of existing OFDM-based systems, where time-frequency samples can be generated using the relationships given in Figure 1 (IWHT and FFT) and then transmitted as OFDM signals. Unlike other single-carrier and OFDM systems, OTSM performs exceptionally well in the presence of Doppler paths, making it suitable for high mobility and underwater acoustic wireless communication.
Due to the use of WHT, if the information symbols are integers, the time-domain samples will take integer values. Therefore, fewer bits can be used to represent the transmission signal without any quantization errors. Benefiting from this feature, OTSM is suitable for an integer forced linear receiver [27].
Channel estimation benefits from the sparse representation of the channel. In OTFS, when the Doppler shift of the channel is an integer multiple of the receiver's Doppler resolution, the delay-Doppler domain provides such a sparse representation. However, in the prevalent case of fractional Doppler shifts, the sparsity significantly decreases. In all cases, the rate domain may provide similar sparsity to that of OTFS with fractional Doppler.

Conclusion We propose OTSM as a new modulation scheme that reuses information symbols on the basis of basic rectangular pulses in both time and rate offset versions, and derive its input-output relationship in the delay-rate domain. By inserting zero padding between each block in the time domain, we propose a low-complexity channel estimation and detection method for OTSM. Simulations show that the performance of OTSM significantly outperforms OFDM and single carrier (SC) transmission, and is comparable to OTFS, but with much lower complexity due to the use of WHT. This makes OTSM a promising solution for future high mobility communication systems requiring low-complexity transceivers. Future work will further analyze the channel representation, low-complexity channel estimation, and detection in the delay-rate domain.
